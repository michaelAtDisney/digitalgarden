# Imagineering backstories

## Definition

Imagineering backstories are the simplistic, whimsical narratives created by Walt Disney Imagineering to explain the themes, designs, and lore of various theme park attractions to the public. Within the context of [[Project Wallick\|Project Wallick]], these accounts are not viewed merely as creative fiction, but as carefully constructed, superficial illusions. Investigators and researchers identify these stories as corporate cover-ups specifically designed to obscure and explain away physical and spatial anomalies occurring within the parks.

## Key Characteristics

- **Superficial Whimsicality:** Narratives are intentionally lighthearted, magical, and highly detailed to capture public interest and distract from physical realities.
- **Structural Masking:** Story elements are mapped directly to physical anomalies (such as non-Euclidean spaces or structural inconsistencies), framing these anomalies as deliberate thematic choices.
- **Corporate Obfuscation:** The narratives serve as a public relations defense mechanism, providing a mundane or fantasy-based explanation for phenomena that would otherwise raise scientific or safety concerns.
- **Plausible Deniability:** By establishing a fictional lore, any structural or environmental deviations experienced by guests can be dismissed as "part of the magic" or show effects.

## Applications

- **Public Relations and Theme Consistency:** Used by [[Walt Disney Parks and Resorts\|Walt Disney Parks and Resorts]] to maintain thematic immersion across different park zones and rides.
- **Anomaly Camouflage:** Applied by corporate operators to hide anomalous spatial or physical deviations from public scrutiny.
- **Project Wallick Investigations:** Utilized by researchers as diagnostic indicators; highly complex or illogical backstories often point directly to the locations of significant underlying spatial anomalies.
