# Expected Historical Record

## Definition

The Expected Historical Record is the official, consensus chronology of historical events that serves as the baseline timeline within [[Project Wallick\|Project Wallick]]. It acts as the standard reference point against which researchers and archival analysis teams monitor, compare, and identify temporal anomalies, timeline shifts, and unauthorized historical incursions.

## Key Characteristics

- **Baseline Control**: Functions as the primary "control" timeline representing accepted human history before any observed anomalies or external interferences.
- **Anomaly Metric**: Used as a diagnostic standard to detect deviations, such as anachronistic technologies, altered historical figures, or modified geopolitical outcomes.
- **Integrity Target**: Preserving and understanding the integrity of this record is a critical directive for containment and analysis teams, serving as the benchmark for successful timeline restoration or preservation.

## Applications

- **Deviation Flagging**: Archival analysis teams cross-reference newly recovered artifacts with the Expected Historical Record. If an artifact shows elements that contradict known history (e.g., modern garments or tools depicted in 18th-century artwork), it is immediately flagged as a major historical deviation.
- **Temporal Incursion Monitoring**: Used by tracking systems to pinpoint the exact historical moments where external interference or timeline manipulation has occurred.
- **Risk Assessment**: Helps determine the severity of a localized temporal anomaly by measuring how far the affected area has drifted from the baseline, often triggering higher protocols like [[Level 4 Analysis\|Level 4 Analysis]].
