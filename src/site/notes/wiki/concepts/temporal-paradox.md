# Temporal Paradox

## Definition

A **Temporal Paradox** is an event or situation that defies chronological consistency, typically characterized by effects preceding their causes or the introduction of future elements into past records. Within research initiatives such as Project Wallick, these paradoxes manifest physically as modern figures and contemporary clothing styles appearing within early 20th-century archival materials.

## Key Characteristics

- **Chronological Inconsistency**: A disruption of linear time where effects may occur before their corresponding causes.
- **Anachronistic Manifestation**: The physical projection or documentation of modern entities, technologies, or fashions in historical contexts where they do not belong.
- **Tri-Factor Causation**: Generally theorized to stem from one of three sources:
  1. Intentional timeline alterations.
  2. Passive timeline bleed-through.
  3. Retrocausal premonitions.
- **Divergence Risk**: If left undocumented or unmanaged, these paradoxes can lead to complete historical divergence, threatening the stability of the baseline timeline.

## Applications

- **Archival Anomalistic Analysis**: Utilized by researchers to detect timeline instability by auditing historical documents for chronological discrepancies.
- **Timeline Stability Management**: Identifying and cataloging paradoxes to prevent the escalation of localized chronological bleeding into widespread reality failures.
